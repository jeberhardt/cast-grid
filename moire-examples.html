Moiré, six more ways

The same dice grid as the main sketch (face die = number of bands, turn die = start corner, invert die = colours), with six different moiré overlays. Each one is controlled by dice too: some add a fourth die per cell, others roll a few “global” dice for the whole artwork. Roll to reroll everything. Open “Code” under any example for a function you can drop into sketch.js.

01Twin grid

A see-through copy of the grid sits on top of itself and slowly twists and zooms. Since both layers use the same dice, the moiré is the grid interfering with itself: giant fringes when the twist passes through zero, finer ones at the extremes. The twist die sets the largest angle in degrees.

02Turntables

      

      

      
Instead of one big disc, every cell gets its own little grating spinning inside it. A fourth spin die per cell sets the speed (×3°/s), and odd turn dice spin clockwise while even ones spin the other way, so neighbouring cells drift in and out of sync.

03Beat fringes

      

      

      
No rotation at all. One grating covers the canvas at 45°, parallel to the bands, with spacing just a hair wider than theirs. The small mismatch creates broad, soft stripes, like the “beat” you hear between two notes that are slightly out of tune. The beat die is roughly how many fringes cross the artwork.

04Decoder

      

      

      
A grating with exactly the band spacing slides one step and back. Tiles started from opposite corners (turn 1/5 vs. 3) sit half a step apart, so they flash solid light and solid dark in turn; turn 2/6 and 4 tiles cross the grating and stay hatched. The overlay “reads” the turn and invert dice that are hidden in the grid.

05Ripples

      

      

      
Two sets of concentric rings, one growing and one shrinking, each centred on a cell chosen by two dice (column, row). Where the rings cross each other you get the classic hyperbolic moiré; where they cross the diagonal bands you get curved fringes that bend around the centres.

06Dot screen

      

      

      
A different tile: the face die sets dot size instead of band count (like halftone printing), and even turn dice stagger the rows. A matching dot screen twists back and forth on top, giving the rosette moiré you see in magnified print. The screen die sets how far it twists.

A few things to know when mixing these in

    
    
      
  • Examples 02–06 draw their overlay with a DIFFERENCE blend (white stripes flip light ↔ dark), so they show on inverted tiles too. To match the current sketch's “black on clear film” look, swap blendMode(DIFFERENCE); fill(255) for fill('#000').
    • 
      
  • The decoder only works when the overlay spacing equals bandSpacing() and bandRatio is 0.5. Any other value gives you beat fringes (03) instead.
    • 
      
  • The overlays are independent, so they stack. Beat fringes + the existing spinning disc, or turntables + ripples, both work well.
    • 
      
  • The snippets use the globals from sketch.js (N, dice, turns, inverts, bandSpacing, bandWidth, drawTile). New dice (spins, twistDie, beatDie, ringA/ringB, screenDie) need to be rolled in rollDice().
